Task decomposition

One assignment is rendered as a short, closed list of time-boxed actions. Progress is stated as a count, never as a percentage that implies you are behind.

Research basis

  • Teevan, Iqbal and von Veh (2016) show that decomposing a large writing task into micro-tasks makes it possible to start without holding the whole task in mind.
  • Sonne et al. (2016) map ADHD challenge areas to concrete assistive strategies: externalise structure rather than demand willpower.
  • McDowall and Kiseleva (2024) find study-skills support is more effective when it is explicit and embedded, not left to the student to discover.

Norman's principles

Constraints

Steps are capped at 15 minutes; the add field splits anything longer automatically.

Consistency

Every row uses the same three-part grammar: checkbox, action, duration. The current row is the only one with a play control.

Feedback

Ticking a row strikes it through and updates the count and meter in the assignment card.

Interaction notes

Tap any row to toggle completion. The play button on the current row carries its task title forward to the focus screen (screen 04). The module segmented control filters the plan.
