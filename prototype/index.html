COMP1649 Human–Computer Interaction · Coursework prototype

NeuroPulse HE

A calm study companion for neurodiverse learners in higher education. Nine standalone mid-fidelity artboards, each researched, specified and annotated with its design rationale.

Screens

01TodayOne next action, an optional energy check-in, and a module list that collapses when the user is overloaded.
02PlanA coursework assignment broken into six steps, each capped at fifteen minutes, with honest progress counts.
03Step detailDefines what "finished" means, supplies ready-made search strings, and offers to split the step again.
04FocusTime as a closing ring and whole minutes; seconds and alarms removed by default. Working countdown.
05Step completeQuiet confirmation, a three-minute rest, and permission to stop for the day. No streaks.
06CalmOverload mode: box breathing, three ways to lower stimulation, and direct routes to human support.
07ReadingLive text size, spacing and typeface controls, with an evidence note explaining why OpenDyslexic is optional.
08SensoryThree palettes applied live, motion and sound switches, and a density control.
09SupportProgress that includes rest days, one honest insight, and consent-based routes to advisers.

Core user journeys

J1 · Start without deciding01 Today → 04 Focus → 05 CompleteShowing only one next action reduces task-initiation latency compared with a full to-do list.
J2 · Make an assignment small enough02 Plan → 03 Step detail → 04 FocusTime-boxed micro-steps plus explicit completion criteria lower perceived workload.
J3 · Adapt when overloaded01 Today (Overloaded) → 06 Calm → 08 Sensory / 07 ReadingIn-the-moment reduction of stimulation and user-controlled typography improve comfort and continued use.

Design rationale

How the research shaped the interface

  • One action per view. Clutter and ambiguous requirements, not task difficulty, are the barriers neurodiverse workers report most often (Morris, Begel and Wiedermann, 2015).
  • Externalised structure. Steps are generated and capped by the system instead of relying on the student's executive function (Sonne et al., 2016; Teevan, Iqbal and von Veh, 2016).
  • No deficit framing. Copy describes support, never correction; every adaptation is reversible and chosen by the user (Spiel et al., 2022).
  • Evidence-led typography. Size and spacing are adjustable; OpenDyslexic is available but not the default, because controlled testing found no reading-rate benefit (Wery and Diliberto, 2017; Rello and Baeza-Yates, 2013).
  • Software is not the whole intervention. The product routes to advisers and wellbeing services (McDowall and Kiseleva, 2024; Van Hees, Moyson and Roeyers, 2015).

Norman's principles in the build

  • Visibility — the next action, the remaining time and the current state are never behind a menu.
  • Feedback — every tap produces a visible state change within the same viewport, in words as well as colour.
  • Constraints — fifteen-minute caps, one primary button per screen, and no "keep going" pressure at completion.
  • Consistency — one token set, one card grammar, one tab bar, across all nine screens.
  • Affordance — 44px minimum targets, iOS-style switches, visible sliders and labelled chevrons.
