### **Assessment Specification** 

**Choose one** of the following topic areas for your coursework: 

- A. Design a digital application (e.g. a mobile app) for **neurodiverse learners** in **a higher education setting** . Potential features may include: task breakdown and time management tools, interface customisation, mood tracking, peer support etc. Accessibility elements could be considered. 

- B. Design a digital application (e.g. a mobile app combined with a smart watch app) for **divers, swimmers, fishers, and sailors to report debris at sea** and other **environmental problems** they encounter on the water. Potential features may include: logging debris sightings, alerting relevant authorities, and sharing information with fellow water sport enthusiasts and volunteer groups. Wearables connected to an app and outdoor interaction design could be considered. 



- C. Design a digital application (e.g. a tablet app or mobile app) for **children** to encourage **reading children’s stories and books** (could be digital books or printed books). Potential features may include: search and reading recommendations, guided (interactive) reading, summarising and quiz activities, progress indicators and rewards, etc. Gamification elements could be considered. 

You have been commissioned to write a report and create a prototype for a new interactive product for one of the above options and target groups. Investigate the needs and context of the chosen target group and the product, and decide on two to three main features, which are supported by your literature research. 

The scope of the interactive prototype should focus on two to three main areas. You are asked to create a proof of concept for the interactions in this system to see if users find it usable and desirable. The basic brief is open for interpretation, and you can and should design desirable interactions as you see fit based on your background readings and your research activities. 

### Interactive prototype 

You need to create a mid-fidelity prototype of the application that enables people to experience at least the core user journeys/scenarios (two or three) that are available in your application in an interactive manner. This prototype should be developed in Axure RP or Figma Design (not Figma Make), unless agreed otherwise with the module leader. 

You are required to describe the _conceptual design_ and provide a description and visualisation of the product and its components and interactions in the report. You need to submit an interactive digital prototype demonstrating the interface and interactions of your application and explain how a user interacts with its components. 

Your design and research activities need to be in alignment with your target group and justifications for all your assumptions and design decisions need to be provided. 

### Report 

At the beginning of your report, you need to state your choice of topic from the above options. In the coursework report, you document your research and design activities, and the required future research study and reflections on your learning. This includes an annotated bibliography that will inform your design, a discussion of the design for the product, and a discussion of how design principles will be applied. The report will also discuss your (design) process of developing the interactive mid-fidelity prototype and how relevant HCI theory has been implemented. A plan for an empirical research study should be proposed to investigate a research question or hypothesis, and the conclusion should reflect on how HCI might influence your future practice. More details can be found in the assessment criteria below. You may also want to consult the 



annotated table of contents available on the COMP1649 Moodle page to help you structure your report. 

Your report needs to be professionally and academically written and structured, based on your own research and reading, and written using appropriate in-text citations and referencing. This includes the demonstration of English language proficiency, appropriate level of detail, professional formatting of the report, and the writing should be supported by at least 8 relevant academic references (journal papers, conference papers, academic books - not blogs or online tutorials etc.). References and in-text citations should be formatted in Harvard style. 

The report word limit is 3000 words. Submissions that exceed this limit by more than 10% will incur a mark deduction. Any content beyond this threshold will not be considered for assessment.

### **Weighted Marking Scheme** 

### Report 

Professional writing style, English language proficiency, writing with appropriate level of detail, professional report formatting, sufficient and appropriate referencing in Harvard style of academic sources (e.g. journal papers, conference papers, academic books) throughout the report. A minimum of 8 sources is expected. **5%** 

An annotated bibliography with reflective commentary of 6-8 relevant academic HCI papers for the chosen coursework topic. The reflection needs to include a personal reflection on why each source was chosen, how it connects to the chosen coursework topic, and what implications can be drawn for the design of the prototype. It should be made clear which academic sources have been used and how they were retrieved. **20%** 

The product design consisting of A) A discussion of the product idea (conceptual design) explaining the components of the product and how the user will interact with the product, and how requirements will be met, and B) The design principles (by Don Norman) and their application to the coursework product are discussed. A brief discussion for each principle/concept and suitable visual representations should be included. **20%** 

A detailed proposal for an empirical HCI research study that uses your interactive prototype. In this proposal, you need to describe and justify the details for a research study including the research question(s) or hypothesis that your research study attempts to investigate, who the participants of your study will be, how the study will be run, the data collection, and how you will analyse the data. You do not need to run the study, but you need to create all necessary material and documentation that are required for a usability expert to run the study. **20%** 

A reflection on one (or more) key insight(s) and how it might influence your future thinking or practice. E.g. Reflect on how your understanding of HCI evolved, what surprised you, or how the topic connects to your own experiences. **5%** 

### Mid-fidelity prototype of an interactive product 

Clear links between the coursework report and the prototype with design decisions explicitly documented and justified in the report. There is evidence of the effective and successful application of HCI theory and design principles to create a prototype that can be used to test core assumptions of your design and that is suitable for researchers and designers to test and evaluate the product. The implementation of design research, theory, and principles is evident in both the report and the prototype. **30%**
